# Find Context

Assemble the minimum context the task needs: not everything that matches, what is actually useful.

## 1. Search broadly

- Search the vault for the terms and their obvious variants.
- Check frontmatter: `type`, `parent`, `tags`.
- Follow wikilinks out from anything that hits; the link graph often says more than the search.
- **Search `04_Archive` too.** Past projects are the most reusable material and the place people forget to look.
- Check `05_Attachments` for source documents whose summaries may not mention the term.

## 2. When the vault comes back thin, look outside it

Only when the vault answer is thin or the question is about an artifact, and stop as soon as it's answered. Cheapest first: the cloud storage folders on disk; cloud documents through their connector; email and calendar through their connectors; earlier agent session transcripts, if available. **Name the layer in the answer:** "the vault says X, the document says Y" is more useful than a blend.

## 3. Rank by usefulness, not match count

A project note that frames the topic beats six notes that mention it in passing. Lead with what orients, then the specifics.

## 4. Report, don't dump

For each relevant note: the wikilink, one line on what it holds, and why it matters here. Then a short synthesis. **Say what you did not find.**

## 5. Offer the next step

If the context reveals something actionable (a stale project, a contradiction, an obvious next action), say so.

## Rules

- Never fabricate. Label general knowledge as such.
- Keep what the vault says separate from what you think.
- With only a handful of hits, read whole notes rather than matched lines.
